In many interfaces, opposing choices look similar but require different amounts of effort. Joining a service might be simple while leaving takes several confirmation steps. ChoiceMirror measures what actually happened in the browser instead of asking an AI to guess.

You are watching a real ChoiceMirror application session. This recording uses genuine Nebius Token Factory inference, with model Z A I G L M five point three Flash. The model reads the controlled webpage, proposes a structured next action, and the application validates that proposal before Playwright executes it. The model cannot simply claim success.

We begin with the asymmetric membership fixture. Both goals start from comparable conditions in a fresh browser context. The model navigates the joining path and the leaving path independently. The app records each event, every counted click, screenshot checkpoints, and model activity.

The finished report shows one counted action to join and three to leave. That is an observed difference of two actions in this synthetic benchmark, not a verdict about any real company's intent.

Next we inspect the evidence. Selecting a recorded step opens the actual browser screenshot and shows what happened at that point. For AI mode, the reported model justification is labelled as model-reported, not independently verified. These distinctions matter because trustworthy measurement depends on traceable sources.

We also run the balanced membership benchmark as a control. Both directions should be equally short. The report is calculated from the actual recorded events, and incomplete or incomparable runs are reported honestly.

ChoiceMirror combines a constrained Nebius navigation agent, isolated Playwright browser execution, structured validation, deterministic comparison metrics and an evidence-linked dashboard.

It currently runs only on the four original synthetic benchmark fixtures, not third-party websites. Its action counts describe these recorded journeys; they are not legal findings.
